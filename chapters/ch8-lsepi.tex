\chapter{Legal, Social, Ethical and Professional Issues}\label{ch:lsepi}

DUET processes account-linked key material, extends the Signal Desktop client, and reports security
evidence that may influence deployment decisions. This chapter examines the resulting legal, social,
ethical, and professional responsibilities.

% ============================================================================
\section{Legal Issues}\label{sec:lsepi-legal}

The client component modifies Signal Desktop, which depends on libsignal. Both upstream projects are
distributed under AGPL-3.0~\cite{libsignal,signal-desktop}. Conveying modified source or object code
must satisfy the applicable licence conditions~\cite[Sections~5--6]{agpl3}. If the modified program
supports remote interaction through a computer network, the GNU AGPL v3 also requires
that users interacting with it be offered the ``Corresponding Source''~\cite[Section~13]{agpl3}. This prototype was
evaluated locally and was not operated as a public service. The prototype retains the upstream
copyright and licence notices. UK guidance treats computer programs and source code as literary
works for copyright purposes~\cite{uk-software-copyright}. Additionally, Signal requires a signed contributor
licence agreement for upstream contributions~\cite{signal-cla}. This route was not taken in this
project.

The project uses publicly documented cryptographic algorithms and open-source implementations.
However, public availability alone does not establish an export-control exemption. UK guidance on
the Cryptography Note states that a product may remain controlled even when its cryptographic
algorithm is publicly specified~\cite{uk-cryptonote}. Therefore, a production release would require
classification against the applicable UK Strategic Export Control List and the guidance current at
that time~\cite{uk-export-lists}.

The key directory stores VRF-derived labels, public-key bindings, warning values, CEA fingerprints,
and epoch metadata. It does not store message content or raw service identifiers. However, the
public registration and lookup endpoints receive raw service identifiers before the server derives
their labels. Pseudonymised data remains personal data when the operator retains the additional
information needed to identify an account~\cite{ico-pseudonymisation}. Therefore, VRF-derived
labels do not make the directory records anonymous to the operator. Production use would require a
documented processing purpose and lawful basis, restricted access to warning records, and defined
retention periods~\cite{ico-lawfulness,ico-data-principles}.

\section{Social and Ethical Issues}\label{sec:lsepi-social}

Undetected key substitution can place users whose safety depends on private communication at risk.
H\"aring et al. examine this setting through an adversarial Signal study in which participants acted
as whistleblowers contacting journalists~\cite{haring2024whistleblower}.
DUET's automatic in-band key-substitution
detection is intended in part to protect such users. Therefore, accurately reporting what was
implemented and its limitations is an ethical obligation. The implementation and
endpoint-compromise boundaries are stated in
\cref{sec:impl-strategy,sec:threat-assumptions}.

Ambiguous, excessive, or privacy-revealing warnings can themselves cause harm. Frequent or poorly
prioritised alerts can cause later warnings, including genuine ones, to be
ignored~\cite{ewaschuk2016monitoring,laubheimer2022alertfatigue}. H\"aring et al. also found that
participants did not always react as intended to an incorrect authentication result, while an
improved interface supported mismatch detection~\cite[Section~4.1]{haring2024whistleblower}.
Therefore, a warning must clearly state its severity
and the action expected from the user. DUET uses amber when the directory is unavailable and red for
a verified integrity or consistency failure, or after the fail-closed operational timeout caused by
three proof-verified peer-slot absences. Usability testing, accessibility, escalation
routes, and support remain future work. Warning records also expose when a warning was published,
creating metadata that requires protection.

The evaluation did not involve human participants. Instead, the live demonstration used two
researcher-controlled Signal accounts connected through Signal's production service and intercepted
no third-party traffic (\cref{ssec:impl-demo}). The benchmark and adversarial inputs were
synthetic. Moreover, the infrastructure, energy, and access costs of operating an independent auditing service
were not measured and would require assessment before deployment.

\section{Professional Issues and Codes of Conduct}\label{sec:lsepi-prof}

The BCS Code of Conduct defines four principles: public interest, professional competence and
integrity, duty to the relevant authority, and duty to the
profession~\cite{bcs-code}. The public interest principle is addressed by considering privacy,
security, and user wellbeing and by stating the prototype's limitations alongside its results.
Professional competence and integrity are supported through established cryptographic libraries,
RFC test vectors, adversarial testing, and the corrected implementation issues documented in
\cref{sec:impl-challenges}. Regular supervisory review and source attribution address the duty to
the relevant authority. Finally, separating measured results from unimplemented production
requirements addresses the duty to the profession.

The IET Rules of Conduct require members to observe the current Statement of Ethical Principles,
maintain their knowledge and skills, work within their competence, and state the limitations of
professional opinions~\cite[Rules~2, 5, 6, and 26]{iet-conduct}. DUET addresses these duties through
RFC conformance tests, explicit assumptions, and qualified security and performance claims. The
implementation defects described in \cref{sec:impl-challenges} were confined to this prototype,
and no defect was found in upstream Signal software. Any upstream vulnerability would be reported through
Signal's security-vulnerability channel~\cite{signal-security-report}.

Appendix~\ref{sec:trace-lsepi} summarizes the responsibilities and project responses discussed in
this chapter.
