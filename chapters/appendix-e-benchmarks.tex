\chapter{Full Benchmark Results}\label{app:benchmarks}

This appendix reproduces the complete committed aggregate benchmark tables summarized in \cref{ch:eval}. All figures are
from a single measurement campaign on the environment recorded below; sizes are exact and
seed-deterministic, and timings are medians (with the 95th percentile and a 95\% confidence
half-width where the harness records them). The metric families M1--M13 are as defined by the
measurement map in \cref{sec:eval-method} (\cref{tab:eval-map}); baselines are named, not coded
(\emph{no-KT floor}, \emph{single-index}, \emph{dual-index}, and \emph{windowed}) and mean the same
thing here as in the chapter. Where a row is qualified \emph{(dual-index)}, it reports the
VRF-indexed \texttt{search\_by\_user\_id} lookup recorded under the historical configuration identifier
\texttt{dual\_index} (see the baseline definitions in \cref{sec:eval-method}). The code$\to$group$\to$CSV mapping for reproducing each row is given in
\cref{sec:bench-repro}.

Several methodology caveats were recorded with the run. The server is in-memory, with no network and
no database. Proof sizes are measured on a single-epoch log, so a deployed $E$-epoch log adds up to
$32\lceil\lg E\rceil$ bytes to each lookup proof. Memory is resident set size after build, not peak.
The operation-rate figures are single-thread per-operation costs. The SHA-NI row is recorded because it bears on
portability rather than being incidental hardware detail: the SHA-256 implementation dispatches to
the hardware instruction at run time, so a host without it takes a software path for every hash, and
the hash-bound operations (the prefix and full verify, detection, the M8 update, M9, and M13)
would be materially slower there, while the VRF-bound operations, where SHA-256 is off the critical
path, would be largely unaffected. The timings below are therefore reported as measurements of this
configuration rather than as properties of the scheme, and pinning the toolchain does not make them
portable. The proof sizes, being computed rather than timed, are hardware-independent.

measures: per-lookup generation and verification latency (\cref{tab:bench-latency}), the marginal
warning-slot lookup cost (\cref{tab:bench-detection}), operation rates and the maintenance operations

\noindent$^{\dagger}$The $10^7$ full-search 95th percentile (\SI{2404.5}{\micro\second}) sits an order
of magnitude above its \SI{141.2}{\micro\second} median, so a small fraction of lookups are far slower
than the typical one. The proof itself does not explain it: the verification steps that consume the
proof (prefix, VRF) show no comparable tail. The harness records no page-fault, paging, or
memory-pressure counters, so the source of the tail was not instrumented and is not established here.
A plausible candidate, given a \num{20786}\,MB working set on a \num{31.7}\,GB machine, is occasional
stalls as the resident set approaches available memory; a deployment on persistent, sharded storage
would have a different memory profile, though whether that removes the tail is untested. The median is
the figure the chapter quotes.

\section{Session versus Continuous Mode (M13)}\label{sec:bench-modes}

The directory-write cost of the two authentication modes over a conversation of $E$ epochs is
compared in \cref{tab:bench-modes}.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption[M13: directory writes per conversation, by mode.]{M13: directory writes over a conversation of $E$ hourly epochs, signed-pre-key
rotation $R=720$.}
\label{tab:bench-modes}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrr@{}}
\toprule
$E$ (epochs) & Continuous writes & Session writes & Ratio \\
\midrule
24    & 24    & 1 & $24.0\times$ \\
168   & 168   & 1 & $168.0\times$ \\
720   & 720   & 1 & $720.0\times$ \\
4{,}320 & 4{,}320 & 6 & $720.0\times$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Per-check verification cost is $27.2\,\si{\micro\second}$ in session mode and
$27.5\,\si{\micro\second}$ in continuous mode (medians; both include server-side proof generation).
The medians differ by $0.3\,\si{\micro\second}$, and that ordering is not interpreted. The
$\pm$ figures the harness records for these two rows ($\pm0.24$ and $\pm0.09\,\si{\micro\second}$)
are confidence half-widths of the \emph{mean} (\cref{sec:bench-env}), so they support no inference
about the medians in either direction; no equivalence claim is made either, since no equivalence
margin was defined or tested. The chain memoization flattens the compressed-leaf
cost that previously separated them.
Both modes are timed under the identical label-bound proof
verifier (\texttt{verify\_search\_result\_raw\_for}), so the two medians are directly comparable under the same local verifier. The
continuous-mode setup folds a simulated per-epoch ratchet secret that is distinct from the
authenticated public ratchet key (ACKA's $I$ versus $T$); it is a stand-in for a live Double-Ratchet
DH output, so the figure captures the transparency-layer cost of an epoch, not the ratchet's own key
agreement.

CSV file/column that records the exact values, so any row above can be regenerated and located. The
harness lives in \texttt{benches/kt\_bench.rs}; run it via \texttt{run-benches.ps1} (the scale family
M7 runs only with \texttt{-ScaleOnly} / \texttt{KT\_BENCH\_LARGE\_N=1}). Sizes are seed-deterministic
and must reproduce byte-for-byte; timings reproduce within noise.

single-thread rate is derived, and \texttt{M11\_handshake\_overhead} measures one complete signed-head

establishment, and the remainder of the messaging-client handshake.
